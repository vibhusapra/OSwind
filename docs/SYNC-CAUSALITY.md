# Sync ordering across objects

Internal development work. F059 remains TODO. This fixes journal ordering needed by complete graph synchronization; it does not provide typed graph/media transfer or user enrollment.

A per-object clock cannot express an observation of another object. For example, device B receives source A1 and then writes chat turn B2 citing it. A third device that has B1 but lacks A1 could previously apply B2 too early. The retained three-library counterexample fails before the fix, including actual chat/source foreign-key admission.

Local puts, deletes and conflict resolutions now include the bounded frontier of every enrolled peer's applied cursor, together with the object's clock. Consequently the receiver parks an ordinary revision until all observed predecessors have applied. An ordinary put whose body has not been admitted does not advance that frontier. The protocol retains its 64-peer bound and refuses fractional/negative cursors and unknown peer observations.

Privacy deletions still take effect before sequence gaps or missing bodies. A separate durable observed clock records these early deletion effects in the same transaction as the deletion barrier, receipt and source callback. Other local objects then carry those observations even after restart. If the callback fails, the observation and receipt roll back together. A delayed old put behind a deletion barrier requires no old body before its cursor can advance.

Explicit internal journal installation migrates old journals by inspecting their bounded pending queue and merging early deletion clocks. It does not scan every source/register. Malformed pending rows refuse the complete transaction without a partial migration. Ordinary library opening remains schema25 with canonical capture disabled. This internal migration is not public enrollment or a compatibility guarantee; trusted enrollment still needs format/version fences.
